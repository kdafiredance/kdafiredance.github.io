How to Make a Kai'Sa FIRESTARTER Vogue Dance Video From One Photo

Kai'Sa's vogue section in the K/DA FIRESTARTER music video is the part everyone keeps replaying: framed arms, precise hands and poses that snap onto the beat. Learning it properly takes practice, but if you just want a clip of yourself (or your cosplay) doing the moves, one good photo is enough. These are the notes I keep for getting a clean result.

1. Understand what the AI can and cannot copy

A photo-to-dance model reads your body from a single image and moves it along a reference routine. Kai'Sa's part suits this well because most of the impact is in the upper body: arm frames, hand shapes and sharp stops. Things the photo never showed, such as your back or a hand hidden behind you, have to be guessed, so give the model as much visible body as possible.

2. Shoot or pick the right photo

  • Full body, head to feet, with a little space around you. Heels and boots in frame.
  • Face the camera. A slight angle is fine; a strong side profile is not.
  • Arms free, not crossed or behind your back. Put the phone, bag or prop down.
  • Plain background and even light. A wall beats a busy convention hall, and coloured stage lighting can blur where your outfit ends.
  • Fitted clothes move more naturally than loose sleeves.
  • One person only. Crop friends out, and only upload people who agreed to be animated.

3. Generate the clip

Upload the photo (JPG, PNG or WEBP) to a Kai'Sa dance AI generator, keep the Kai'Sa Vogue template selected and press generate. The result is a 12-second 16:9 MP4 at 720p with the matching part of the FIRESTARTER song already synced to the moves. Pricing on that site is a one-time pack starting at $9.90 for one generation, with no subscription, and the uploaded photo is deleted automatically after 24 hours.

4. Crop to 9:16 for TikTok, Reels and Shorts

A 16:9 frame cropped to 9:16 keeps only about a third of the width, so placement matters:

  1. In CapCut (or your phone's editor) set the canvas to 9:16.
  2. Scale the clip so the dancer fills the height, then centre them. Scrub through all 12 seconds: if an arm frame leaves the crop, nudge the position or add a keyframe.
  3. Trim any dead frames at the start so the first pose lands immediately.
  4. Keep the bottom of the frame clear for captions.

5. Post it responsibly

  • Turn on the AI-generated content label when you upload.
  • Credit the routine to the K/DA FIRESTARTER video and its choreographers, and credit your photographer if someone else shot your cosplay.
  • Want to appear on the trend's sound page? Lower the original audio and add the official FIRESTARTER sound in the app.
  • A fun follow-up: post your own attempt next to the AI version, using Riot's mirrored choreography video to practise in eight-count blocks.
